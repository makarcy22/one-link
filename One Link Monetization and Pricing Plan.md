# ONE LINK

## Monetization, Pricing, Marketing, and Excel Analysis Plan

**Product:** One Link
**Goal:** Turn “Create once. Reach everywhere” into a sustainable creator and business SaaS product.
**Primary loop:** Create → Optimize → Discover → Publish → Monitor → Improve

All prices and financial figures in this file are **illustrative planning assumptions**, not final prices or cost quotes.

---

## 1. Monetization Strategy

### 1.1 Core strategy

Use a **freemium SaaS model with usage-based add-ons**.

- Free users validate the workflow.
- Paying creators fund ongoing usage.
- Businesses pay more for capacity, accounts, collaboration, and reporting.
- Heavy AI, video, storage, and publishing users pay through add-ons rather than raising prices for everyone.

### 1.2 Revenue streams

#### A. Subscriptions

Primary MVP revenue:

- Free.
- Creator.
- Business.

#### B. Usage-based add-ons

Protect margins from heavy users:

- AI credit packs.
- Additional connected accounts.
- Additional brand profiles.
- Additional storage.
- Higher publishing or scheduling volume.
- Longer analytics history.
- Priority media processing.

#### C. Annual plans

Sell annual billing after monthly retention is proven:

- Monthly billing for flexibility.
- Annual billing for improved cash flow and lower churn.

#### D. Post-MVP expansion

Add only after retention and reliability are proven:

- Team plans.
- Agency and client workspaces.
- Approval workflows.
- Campaign management.
- Advanced reporting.
- Template marketplace revenue sharing.
- Influencer collaboration tools.
- Premium trend intelligence.

Avoid advertising as early revenue. It can damage creator trust before retention is strong.

## 2. Recommended MVP Pricing Structure

### 2.1 Pricing tiers

| Plan | Illustrative monthly price | Best for | Core limits |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Free | $0 | Testing One Link | 1 brand, limited accounts, limited AI, limited storage, limited scheduled posts |
| Creator | $12–$25 | Individual creators and personal brands | More AI generations, more scheduled posts, larger storage, longer analytics history |
| Business | $39–$99 | Businesses and serious creators | Multiple brands or locations, more accounts, higher publishing volume, priority processing, expanded analytics |

Final prices should be tested against:

- Conversion rate.
- Churn.
- Support cost.
- AI and video cost per user.
- Competitor pricing and positioning.

### 2.2 Usage add-ons

| Add-on | Purpose | When to buy |
|---|---|---|
| AI credit pack | Extra captions, ideas, hooks, hashtags, assistant use | User exceeds monthly AI allowance |
| Extra social account | Connect another TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, or other supported account | Creator manages multiple channels |
| Extra brand profile | Manage another business, client, or personal brand | Business or future agency use |
| Storage expansion | Store more video, audio, images, and graphics | Heavy media libraries |
| Publishing-volume expansion | Schedule or publish more posts | High-frequency creators |
| Analytics-history expansion | Retain older performance data | Businesses needing reporting |
| Priority processing | Faster media transcoding and publishing queue | Time-sensitive campaigns |

### 2.3 Free-plan design

The free plan should allow a complete but limited journey:

- Create content.
- Upload existing content.
- Generate a limited number of captions and hashtags.
- Connect a limited number of accounts.
- Publish or schedule a limited number of posts.
- View basic analytics.
- Receive limited recommendations.

Free-plan quotas must specifically control:

- AI generations.
- Video minutes processed.
- Storage used.
- Publishing volume.
- Analytics refresh frequency.

These scenarios assume free-user costs are controlled through quotas. They do not include salaries, marketing spend, app development costs, taxes, payment-processing fees, or customer-support labor.

### 3.2 Break-even formula

Use this formula in Excel:

```text
Break-even paying users =
Monthly fixed and operating costs ÷
Average revenue per paying user after variable costs
```

Example logic:

- Higher fixed costs require more paying users.
- Higher variable AI/video costs require higher prices or stricter limits.
- Annual plans reduce the number of new customers needed every month.
- A small improvement in free-to-paid conversion can matter more than adding many additional free users.

### 3.3 Variables to change in Excel

Test changes to:

- Total users.
- Free-to-paid conversion rate.
- Monthly ARPU.
- Annual-plan percentage.
- AI cost per user.
- Video-processing cost per user.
- Storage and bandwidth cost.
- Fixed hosting and service costs.
- Support cost per active user.
- Payment-processing percentage.

The most sensitive variables are usually:

1. Free-to-paid conversion.
2. AI cost per active user.
3. Video-processing cost per active user.
4. Churn.

## 4. Marketing Plan

### 4.1 Positioning

Primary message:

> **ONE LINK — Create once. Reach everywhere.**

Supporting messages:

- Your social media. One place.
- Create. Trend. Publish. Grow.
- One creation. Everywhere.
- Everything you need to create and grow online.

Marketing should emphasize outcomes, not features:

- Less switching between apps.
- Faster publishing.
- Better captions and hashtags.
- Clearer performance insight.
- Better ideas for the next post.

### 4.2 Target audiences

#### Audience 1: Independent creators

Motivations:

- Grow followers.
- Save editing and publishing time.
- Find trends earlier.
- Improve hooks, captions, and hashtags.

Best channels:

- TikTok.
- Instagram Reels.
- YouTube Shorts.
- Creator communities.
- Live demonstrations.

#### Audience 2: Small businesses

Motivations:

- Promote products and services.
- Stay consistent online.
- Avoid hiring a full social-media team immediately.
- Understand what content works.

Best channels:

- Local business communities.
- Facebook and Instagram.
- WhatsApp business communities where appropriate.
- Email marketing.
- Partnerships with agencies, photographers, and marketers.

### 4.3 Launch stages

#### Stage 1: 10 design partners

- Manually onboard creators and businesses.
- Record onboarding calls and friction points.
- Publish early case studies.
- Fix publishing reliability before broader marketing.

#### Stage 2: 100 private-beta users

- Use invitations or a waitlist.
- Offer a founding-member discount.
- Require feedback in exchange for lower pricing.
- Track activation, repeat publishing, conversion, churn, and support load.

#### Stage 3: 1,000 users

Scale marketing only after:

- Publishing success is reliable.
- Unit economics are understood.
- AI and media costs are predictable.
- Support processes can handle growth.

Recommended acquisition methods:

- Creator demonstrations.
- Before-and-after publishing workflows.
- Trend-to-content examples.
- Templates and hooks.
- Referral incentives.
- Business onboarding webinars.
- Partnerships with creator communities and small-business groups.

### 4.4 Content marketing pillars

1. Content ideas and hooks.
2. Caption and hashtag education.
3. Platform-specific publishing guides.
4. Trend breakdowns.
5. Performance case studies.
6. Small-business promotion playbooks.
7. Creator productivity workflows.

Every content asset should lead to:

- A template.
- A trend.
- A “Create This” workflow.
- A free trial or waitlist signup.

### 4.5 Referral and retention marketing

- Reward users for inviting creators or businesses.
- Send personalized performance summaries.
- Notify users about relevant trends in their niche.
- Recommend the next post based on prior performance.
- Re-engage inactive users with saved drafts and content ideas.

## 5. Examples and Lessons from Other Apps

The examples below describe common monetization patterns, not One Link competitors’ current prices or private business metrics.

### 5.1 Design and template tools

Common pattern:

- Free templates and basic creation.
- Paid plans for premium templates, brand kits, collaboration, and higher limits.

Lesson for One Link:

- Templates lower the barrier to creation.
- Brand kits and saved assets justify business pricing.
- Collaboration and approvals belong in higher tiers.

### 5.2 Social-media schedulers

Common pattern:

- Free or low-cost entry plan.
- Higher tiers for more accounts, users, scheduling volume, analytics, and reporting.

Lesson for One Link:

- Do not give unlimited publishing or accounts to low-priced plans.
- Charge separately for additional profiles and volume.
- Analytics history is a strong paid incentive.

### 5.3 Creator video and editing tools

Common pattern:

- Free core editing.
- Paid plans for premium effects, higher export quality, cloud features, commercial rights, or AI features.

Lesson for One Link:

- Keep basic creation accessible.
- Charge for AI-heavy and media-heavy features.
- Performance, exports, priority processing, and commercial workflows can support premium pricing.

### 5.4 Freelance and creator marketplaces

Common pattern:

- Free participation.
- Commission, service fees, premium placement, or business subscriptions.

Lesson for One Link:

- A future creator marketplace should take a transaction or subscription fee.
- Do not build a marketplace before supply, demand, trust, payments, disputes, and support processes exist.

### 5.5 Business productivity SaaS

Common pattern:

- Per-user or per-workspace pricing.
- Higher tiers for permissions, audit logs, reporting, integrations, and support.

Lesson for One Link:

- Future agency pricing should be based on workspaces, clients, seats, approvals, and reporting.
- Audit and permission features are valuable to businesses and agencies.

## 6. Recommended Path from 100 to 1,000 Users

1. Validate the full product loop with 10 design partners.
2. Expand to 100 beta users with strict usage quotas.
3. Measure conversion, churn, support burden, publishing reliability, and AI costs.
4. Adjust pricing and free-plan limits.
5. Improve onboarding and documentation.
6. Add cost alerts and abuse controls.
7. Expand to 1,000 users only after unit economics are sustainable.
8. Invest in automation, CDN performance, background workers, and customer support.

The safest sequence is:

> **Prove workflow → prove repeat usage → prove willingness to pay → control variable costs → scale.**
